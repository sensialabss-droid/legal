Privacy Policy — Flying Piggy Bank

Last updated: 11 November 2026 · Español · Français · Deutsch · Italiano · Português (Brasil)

SensiaLabss (Spain) develops Flying Piggy Bank (the "App"; in Spanish, Hucha Voladora), a money education app for children aged 6 to 12, designed to be used with an adult. The App does not move real money: the child writes down what comes in and what goes out, decides before spending and plays "The Savings Leap"; an adult acts as the "bank". This policy explains to parents and guardians what data the App handles and where it stays. Contact: sensialabss@gmail.com.

In short: everything that is written down or earned in the App stays on the device. There are no accounts, no sign-up, no servers of ours, no analytics, no ads and no chat. The App does not ask for the child's real name, date of birth, email, phone number, location or photos: there is no field for any of that. It only asks, if the adult wishes, for a nickname and which age the game questions are for (6 to 8 or 9 to 12). We do not receive anything that is stored in the App.
For kids, in a few words: what you write down in Flying Piggy Bank stays on this phone. We can't see it. There are no ads and no chat, and nobody can message you from the App. The game tokens are pretend: they are not money. If there's something you don't understand, ask a grown-up at home.

1. What the App stores and where

In the App's private storage, only on that device:

  • money entries (amount, date, category and any text that is typed);
  • wishes, the things the child is thinking over before buying, chores and the allowance;
  • the jar split and the achievements on the wall, which make up the album;
  • the nickname of each profile and which age the game questions are for, if the adult sets them;
  • from the game "The Savings Leap": the record, the game tokens, the trophies and ranks won against the villains, the chosen character and the tricks the companion has already learned;
  • from the journey map: the tokens put into each place's piggy bank, the questions answered correctly and the treasures found;
  • from the token shop: what has been obtained with tokens (hats, trails, furniture for the house and the "Coin rain" mini-game), what each character is wearing, the furniture on display, the mini-game record and the tokens it has given that day;
  • some game usage counters for the adult (for example, how many times the game has been played, how many flights, how many metres in total, how many fights were won or passed). They only add up, they are shown in the parents' area and they never leave the device: they are not analytics and they do not reach us;
  • the App's settings, including the language (Spanish, English, French, German, Italian or Portuguese) if an adult chooses it in the parents' area and, for families who were already using the App before it had more languages, the currency their entries are in (euros), so that it does not change;
  • a random code that the App itself creates the first time, to count separately the tokens earned on each device (for example, on a phone and on a tablet). It is not the phone's identifier or the advertising identifier, it says nothing about who uses the App and it does not leave the device, except as a label for those tokens inside the backup;
  • a receipt for the purchase of the Pro version, if it has been bought (see section 5);
  • if the adult turns them on: the parents' area PIN and its recovery question.

To suggest a language and a currency, the App checks, on the phone itself, the language and region the phone is set to (for example, "English, United Kingdom" is shown in English and in pounds, and "Portuguese, Brazil" in Portuguese and in reais). This information is used inside the phone and does not leave it.

There is no syncing and no cloud copy. Android's automatic backup and automatic transfer of data to another phone are turned off on purpose.

2. What the App does NOT handle

The App does not ask for or store the child's surname, date of birth, exact age, email, phone number, address, location, photos, voice, contacts or advertising identifiers.

The only exceptions are the nickname of each profile and the age of the game questions. The adult can set them in the first-minute setup, which is behind the adult check (both are optional), and the App asks for the nickname when there is more than one profile, so that siblings don't mix up their notebooks. The nickname can be anything (the real name isn't needed and we recommend not using the full name); the age is only a range (6 to 8 or 9 to 12) to choose the questions. Both can be changed from the parents' area, are stored only on the device, are deleted with "Delete all data" and we do not receive them.

There is no chat, no public profile, no way for the child to talk to other people and no links to websites from the child's screens.

3. System permissions

  • Notifications: "Let me think" reminders. They are local: the phone itself schedules them, they don't go through any server and, if the phone restarts, the system schedules them again. They may show the profile nickname and the name of the wish on the phone's screen. They can be turned off in Android settings.
  • Vibration: touch feedback in the game.
  • Fingerprint (optional): to open the parents' area. The system itself does the check; the App only gets a "yes" or a "no" and never sees or stores biometric data.
  • In-store purchases: to buy the Pro version from the parents' area and get it back after changing phones.
  • Internet access: used only by the app store, for two things: handling the purchase of the Pro version and telling the App whether there is a new version (the update notice is only shown in the parents' area). The App does not send through it any of the data stored inside.

The App does not ask for camera, microphone, location, contacts, calendar or photo access.

4. Backup

An adult can export a copy from Settings, which are opened with the parents' PIN or, if no PIN has been set, with an adult check. The copy contains the App's data (entries, wishes, chores, achievements, nicknames, settings and game progress, including the map and the token shop, and counters). It does not include the PIN, the recovery question and answer, or the purchase status. The file only leaves the device the way the adult chooses to share it; we do not receive it and have no access to it.

5. Purchases

Flying Piggy Bank costs nothing to download and has a one-time payment Pro version, with no subscription. The purchase can only be made from the parents' area, which is opened with the parents' PIN or, if no PIN has been set, with an adult check; no money price or buy-with-money button appears on the child's screens. (What the child "buys" in the game's token shop is paid only with tokens earned by playing: it costs no money.) Payment for the Pro version is handled entirely by the app store with the account on the device, which in many countries (including those of the European Union and the United Kingdom) acts as the seller, and which handles the purchase data under its own privacy policy: we do not see or store payment details, or the buyer's name or email. The App keeps a receipt of the purchase on the device to know it has been paid for, and does not send it to anyone.

The store does provide us with sales reports showing the order number, product, date, amount and country of each purchase (and, in some cases, the province or postal code). We use them only to keep our accounts and meet our legal obligations, we keep them for as long as those rules require, and we store them in protected accounts that only we can access, from Spain. This is data about the purchase made by the adult, not about the child.

6. An honest note

The purchase library needed to take payments through the store, and the one that asks the store whether there is a new version, send the store technical information about how they work. They are the store's own code, they come with it and they carry none of the data entered in the App. We say this because "absolutely nothing is sent" would not be accurate.

7. Children

The App is aimed at children aged 6 to 12 and designed to be used with an adult. That is why it is built not to need any personal data about the child and to keep everything on the device. It has no ads, no analytics and no third-party tools that collect data, and it does not use the device's advertising identifier. The adult features (purchase, backup, restore, language and deleting data) are behind the parents' PIN or, if none has been set, an adult check.

Because we do not collect personal data about the child, we do not ask the child or the adult for consent. If that ever changed, we would tell you first and ask for consent from the person with parental responsibility, as the law of each country requires for children under the age it sets.

8. If you write to us, and your rights

If an adult writes to us at sensialabss@gmail.com, we will use their email address and what they tell us only to reply, and we will delete it once it is no longer needed for the enquiry. Please do not send us any data about the child; if we receive it, we will delete it. For those emails and for the sales reports, you can ask us at any time for access, rectification, erasure, objection or restriction by writing to that same address and, if you are not satisfied with our reply, you can complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD, www.aepd.es) or to the data protection authority of your country.

9. How to delete the data

From Settings, with "Delete all data", or by uninstalling the App. No copy is left anywhere of ours, because we have no servers and Android's automatic backup is turned off. Any backups the adult has exported are deleted by the adult.

10. Families in other countries

European Union. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies. The data controller is in Spain; you can exercise your rights as explained in section 8 and complain to the data protection authority of your country (for example, the CNIL in France, your federal state's authority or the federal one in Germany, or the Garante in Italy).

United Kingdom. The UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) Age Appropriate Design Code apply. That is why the App is designed not to collect data about the child, with no ads, no profiling, no location and no chat. You can exercise your rights as explained in section 8 and complain to the ICO (ico.org.uk).

United States. We do not collect personal information from children within the meaning of the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA): what is written in the App stays on the device and is never sent to us; the App has no ads, analytics or tracking, and it does not send us any identifier of the device or of the child. Because it does not track anyone across websites or apps, "Do Not Track" signals make no difference. Parents and guardians can see everything the App stores and delete it whenever they want (section 9). If you believe a child has sent us personal information, for example by email, write to us and we will delete it.

Brazil. The General Data Protection Law (LGPD, Law 13,709/2018) applies. What is stored in the App stays on the family's device and is not processed by us. We only process the email of anyone who writes to us and the sales reports (sections 5 and 8), from Spain, to reply, keep our accounts and meet legal obligations. The channel for exercising your rights (access, correction, deletion and the others the LGPD recognises) is sensialabss@gmail.com; you can also contact the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD, www.gov.br/anpd).
